# Source scan, 12 September 2026

Out of cycle. Two things launched this week that the recurring process exists to catch:
computepulse.net and computeindex.io, both live GPU pricing trackers. Neither survives the
admission screen as a source. Computeindex.io's coverage page is a name-harvest channel
nobody had read yet, and computepulse's own history endpoint turned out to be worth taking
anyway, just not as a price input.

## Two competitors, two different failure modes

**ComputePulse** publishes its methodology in full: a weighted trimmed mean, 10% trimmed
off each tail, 25% provider weight cap, confidence x recency weighting, 24-hour staleness.
It names exactly three sources it treats as live (Azure retail, Shadeform, Vast.ai)
against a global unit. AWS, CoreWeave, Lambda, RunPod, GCP and Oracle are shown on the
site and excluded from the computation, labelled static rate cards. Two of its three live
inputs are already this project's inputs. It fails gate 3 as a price (no region breakdown
at all, since the unit is global) and gate 5 (aggregator).

What it does have is an undocumented, unauthenticated endpoint its own front end calls:

```
GET https://www.computepulse.net/api/indices/history?slug=h100-global-compute&timeframe=all
```

176 daily buckets back to 2026-03-21, 2,987 raw observations behind them, today's value
$2.7881. `blackwell-global-compute` is the same shape, $6.1652 today. Both gap honestly:
27 and 28 explicit nulls rather than carried-forward values. That is a real, if noisy,
independent series, and `tci.validate.check_series_correlation` already exists to consume
exactly this kind of thing against `config/check_series.csv`, which has never been
populated. Recorded as a `watchlist` cross-check, not a source. The file is empty and
stays that way until someone writes the fetch script and decides on a refresh cadence.

**ComputeIndex** is the more direct competitor. CI-CPI v2 is currently publishing
nothing: its own coverage page says 0 of 5 GPU families cleared the live-provider-depth
gate, and of the 19 providers it lists, only RunPod and Vast.ai are marked "Live." The
other 17 are "Reference" rate cards, the same problem this project has, stated more
starkly. Its robots.txt disallows `/api/` for every user agent and blocks GPTBot, CCBot,
ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider and cohere-ai outright, and its data products are
CSV/Parquet/API under "institutional license" only. Gate 4 fails before gate 1 is worth
checking. Recorded `watchlist`, same shelf as silicon_data.

**ComputePrices.com**, already on the register, gets one update. Its API terms now
explicitly prohibit "building competing pricing services" in addition to the existing
Bearer-key wall. The rejection stops being contingent on an unauthenticated endpoint
appearing: gate 4 would fail on principle even if gate 1 didn't.

## What ComputeIndex's coverage page named

Eight providers on that list have no row here: CUDO Compute, FluidStack, Oblivus, Ori,
Paperspace, TensorDock, Together AI, Vultr. Screened all eight.

**Vultr** is the closest to a real candidate and the cleanest failure of the batch.
`GET https://api.vultr.com/v2/plans?type=all` is a genuine public API, no key, the same
one the console reads, with `hourly_cost`, `gpu_count` and `locations` on every plan. It
clears gates 1, 4 and 5 without qualification. The 15 GPU plans it returns today are all
NVIDIA_A16, A40 or L40S; nothing H100-class exists in the product line, and all but the
two cheapest A16 SKUs carry an empty `locations` array. Fails gate 2 cleanly, and would
fail gate 3 too even if it didn't.

**CUDO Compute** fails gate 2 the same way Gcore did: every GPU on the pricing page,
including H100, reads "Quote on request." **TensorDock** publishes real prices on its own
marketing page (H100 SXM5 from $2.25/hr), but the read side of its API is unresolved
rather than gated. The unauthenticated v0 hostnodes endpoint returns
`{"success":true,"hostnodes":{}}`, empty, and POST is 405. That is not the same shape as a
Bearer-key wall; it might be a wrong path, or it might need a key nobody documents for
reads. Left as `watchlist` with a recheck rather than folded into computeprices'
rejection.

**Together AI** publishes real on-demand cluster rates, HGX H100 $3.99, HGX H200 $5.99,
HGX B200 $8.19, all per GPU-hour, and fails gate 3 exactly like GPU.ai: no region or
country appears anywhere on the pricing page. **Ori** turned out to already be gone: its
`/pricing` URL redirects to radiant.co, the result of a merger with Brookfield's AI
infrastructure arm, and the surviving page has no rate at all, sales-quote only.
**Paperspace** fails on two fronts at once. It is DigitalOcean-owned, and this project
already has a DigitalOcean collector in shadow under DigitalOcean's own name, so a second
collector for the same operator is the gate-5 double-counting problem this register exists
to prevent. Its headline H100 figure is also labelled "special promo price," not a stated
on-demand rate. **FluidStack**'s `/pricing` 404s on both `fluidstack.io` and
`www.fluidstack.io` as of today, unverified rather than rejected.

## Two more names with real prices and no location, found independently

Not from either competitor's list. **Massed Compute** (H100 $2.73/hr, H200 NVL $3.62/hr,
A100 $1.35/hr) and **Denvr Dataworks** (H100 $2.45/GPU, A100 $1.45/GPU) both publish
genuine on-demand per-GPU rates and name no country or region anywhere on their pricing
pages or homepages. Both fail gate 3 in the same shape as GPU.ai and Together AI. Three
independent sources hitting the identical failure mode this month says something about how
this market publishes prices: rate first, geography never, because most buyers renting a
single GPU don't care which building it's in.

## Oblivus: the wrong product at the right address

Oblivus was worth a close look because its pricing page names an EEA location, OSL1,
Oslo, next to the more usual MON1, Montreal. Every H100- and A100-class deploy link on the
page is pinned to MON1. OSL1 carries exactly one GPU: RTX_4090, a consumer card outside
`factors.yaml:model_classes`. The Oslo site sells the wrong product. Real prices (H100
SXM5 $4.31/hr, A100 NVLINK $1.57/hr), an EEA address, and the two do not meet on the same
page. Recorded `watchlist` with the specific recheck: an H100- or A100-class SKU offered
at OSL1, not just at MON1.

## Nscale: no price yet, but the deepest EEA footprint found this sweep

Nscale is sales-quote only: "Reserve GPUs" routes to a contact form, no pricing page
exists. Kept because its homepage names more EEA and EFTA sites than any other lead this
sweep: Glomfjord, Narvik, Stavanger and Oslo in Norway; Keflavik and Blonduos in Iceland;
Sines in Portugal; plus Loughton and Hayes in the UK, which would land in the shadow block
only. If this one ever publishes a rate, it is a candidate for the published headline
directly rather than 180 days in a shadow block first.

## Cherry Servers: a real public API that doesn't cover the product

The cleanest gate-1 pass of the sweep, and the one I'm most reluctant to close out
cleanly. `GET https://api.cherryservers.com/v1/plans` needs no key, is documented, and
returns `available_regions` and `pricing` per plan, exactly the shape a stranger could
rebuild a print from. The operator's own footer names Lithuania, Netherlands, Germany and
Sweden as locations, all EEA. But the 82 plans that endpoint returns today are vps, vds,
baremetal and storage-vps, no GPU SKU among them. The GPU catalog (A100, A40, A16, A10,
A2, Tesla P4; nothing H100-class) lives on a separate page, `/dedicated-gpu-servers`,
which is server-rendered with no price digits anywhere in the raw response I fetched. A
tool call against that same URL reported back "A100 EUR2.019/hr," which I could not
reproduce against the raw HTML and am not recording as a confirmed number. Flagging the
discrepancy here rather than quietly trusting it. Recheck is specific: either the GPU
plans start appearing in the public `/v1/plans` response, or someone finds where that
page's price actually comes from.

## What the register looks like now

53 rows, up from 39: eight live, one built and blocked on a token, thirteen shadow,
twenty-eight watchlist, three rejected. Nothing added to the panel; nothing in the
calculation path changed.

## Next

Vultr and Cherry Servers are the two worth another look before the next scheduled sweep.
Both clear the hard gates (1, 4, 5) and fail only on product fit, which is exactly the
kind of thing that changes with a new SKU rather than a new company. TensorDock's read API
is a half-hour of documentation-reading, not a rewrite, if anyone wants to resolve it
before it sits stale. Everything else here is a rediscovery-diff candidate for October,
not urgent work now.
